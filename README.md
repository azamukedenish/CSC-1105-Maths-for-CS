# CSC 1105: Mathematics for Computer Science

Lecture slides, study materials, labs, practical tutorials, assignments, and supporting resources for **CSC 1105 - Mathematics for Computer Science**.

This course introduces the mathematical foundations needed for advanced computer science topics such as image processing, machine learning, computer graphics, interface design, robotics, and security. It extends pre-university mathematics with a focus on concepts and techniques that help students reason about computational systems.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Compute limits of polynomial, logarithmic, exponential, trigonometric, and mixed functions.
2. Establish continuity and differentiability of functions.
3. Calculate the inverse, determinant, and transpose of matrices.
4. Perform 2D and 3D transformations using matrices.
5. Perform vector and vector space algebra.
6. Generate mathematical characteristics involving vectors and planes.

## Course Modules

| Module | Topic | Contact Hours | Main Focus |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Limits and Continuity | 10 | Limits, limit laws, squeeze theorem, L'Hopital's rule, limits at infinity, asymptotes, continuity, differentiability, derivatives, chain rule, and rates of change. |
| 2 | Progressions and Sequences | 10 | Arithmetic and geometric progressions, special progressions, finite and infinite sequences, sequence limits, and upper/lower bounds. |
| 3 | Matrix Algebra | 10 | Matrix rules, transposes, inverses, determinants, eigenvectors, kernels, transformations, and solving systems of equations. |
| 4 | Vectors and Vector Spaces | 15 | Vector operations, linear combinations, spanning sets, linear dependence, independence, and orthogonality. |
| 5 | Vectors and Planes | 15 | Vector equations of lines in n-space, equations of planes, angles, distances, and relationships between vectors and planes. |

## Labs And Practical Tutorials

The `labs/` folder is for practical mathematics tutorials that help students apply course concepts to computer science contexts.

Lab themes include:

- Limits, continuity, and rates of change in computational models.
- Sequences and progressions in growth patterns, iteration, and algorithm analysis.
- Matrix operations for transformations, graphics, and systems of equations.
- Vector spaces for data representation, geometry, and machine learning foundations.
- Lines, planes, distances, and angles in 2D, 3D, and n-dimensional spaces.

## Assessment

Assessment follows the curriculum structure:

- Progressive assessment: 40%
  - Each module should include at least one assignment and one test.
- Final examination: 60%
  - The final exam is completed at the end of the semester.

## Mode Of Delivery

The course uses a blended, learner-centered approach. Sessions may include face-to-face teaching, online delivery, guided reading, prepared tutorials, group discussions, discussion forums, and webinars.
